Security vendor Ox Security analyzed 542,207 open source software components between July and October 2026 and found that, beyond classic malicious code, a growing share of attacks rely on "tool poisoning" and prompt injection, text crafted to convince an AI agent to misuse permissions it already holds. For a business connecting AI agents to outside tools, the research shows that security review can no longer just check whether a tool's code is malicious, it must also check who controls that tool, where it runs, and whether someone can quietly change its instructions later.

Italy's data protection authority fines Meta EUR 805 million over access to users' Facebook photo galleries

Italy's data protection authority (Garante) fined Meta Platforms Ireland EUR 805 million, one of the largest GDPR fines of the year, following an investigation opened after a 2025 consumer complaint. The case centered on a Facebook feature that surfaced photos and videos stored on a user's phone as suggestions for posting. The authority found that the app requested a single bundled permission to access the photo gallery for several different features at once, without clearly disclosing that the permission would also enable this specific feature, which was turned on by default. Garante ruled that Meta had not obtained specific, informed consent for this use, in breach of Article 122 of Italy's data protection code, and rejected Meta's argument that the case should have been handled under the GDPR's one-stop-shop mechanism. For any business requesting device-level data access through an app, the case shows how strictly European regulators now distinguish between a blanket permission and genuinely informed consent for each individual use.

OpenAI's chief scientist proposes a shared "speed limit" for AI model development

Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI's chief scientist, published a post proposing a way to measure a possible shared limit on how fast AI models improve, a proposal CEO Sam Altman called "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while." The post follows Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's proposal to "pace the frontier" of AI progress, which both Altman and Elon Musk publicly endorsed. Pachocki proposes perplexity as the shared measure, a metric of how well a model predicts the next word in a piece of text, arguing that unlike capability benchmarks that quickly become outdated, perplexity has stayed a reliable point of comparison over time. According to the post, once that metric fell below a certain threshold in the company's own models, strong capabilities in fields such as cybersecurity and mathematics emerged abruptly, and the proposal is for AI companies to commit not to exceed a jointly agreed threshold until they develop enough ability to monitor model behavior. For a business tracking where AI regulation is heading, the proposal shows that AI companies themselves now recognize the need for measurable limits on model development, though it does not replace binding legislation or independent oversight.
